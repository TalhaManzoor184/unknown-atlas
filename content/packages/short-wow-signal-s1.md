## Voiceover script

**[HOOK]** In August 1977, an astronomer sat reviewing computer paper and saw a sequence that stopped him cold.

**[INTRO]** Volunteer Jerry Ehman was checking data from Ohio State University's Big Ear radio telescope.
  _Pronunciation: Jerry Ehman = EE-man_

**[CONTEXT]** The telescope listened for extraterrestrial signals, recording signal intensity on paper using numbers and letters whenever radio signals rose above background noise.

**[EVIDENCE]** Most lines were quiet noise. Then came six characters: 6EQUJ5.

**[TWIST]** It was not a decoded alien sentence, but an intensity scale. At its peak, the signal reached thirty standard deviations above background noise.

**[CONCLUSION]** Ehman circled it on the printout and wrote Wow. It lasted seventy-two seconds, and space never repeated it. Follow for more strange truths from our universe.

## Description
In August 1977, volunteer astronomer Jerry Ehman noticed a 72-second radio spike while reviewing data from Ohio State's Big Ear telescope. Circling the intensity code '6EQUJ5', he wrote 'Wow!' in the margin—and the cosmos has kept silent ever since.

## Fact-check rounds
- Round 1: **revise**, 3 issue(s)
  - [medium] scene 3: printing letters whenever radio noise spiked — The printout represented intensity values using alphanumeric characters where numbers 1 to 9 represented intensities from 1 to 9, and letters were only used for intensities of 10 and above, not whenever radio noise spiked.
  - [medium] scene 5: At its peak, the signal reached thirty times background noise. — The text states that the highest measured value 'U' represents an intensity thirty standard deviations above background noise (signal-to-noise ratio), not thirty times background noise.
  - [high] scene 6: Ehman circled it in red pen — The source articles state that Ehman circled the reading on the computer printout, but do not mention that the pen was red.
